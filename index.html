How to Play

You are Kindle, a young dragon who wanders into the Devil’s maze (player 2 is Cinder). The Devil on top points, and his imps crank the whole maze that way. The frame around the maze is solid: if it pushes you into a wall, you are crushed. A red glow marks the edge that is closing in.

Each round has three scenes

  1. Scene 1 — Magic dots. Walk over a cross to hold it. Only while you hold a cross can you eat dots and breathe fire. The cross fades after a while (your meter flashes), and its marker refills later. Clear every dot. Burnt slimes turn into a toasted puff worth 500; four candy apples (800) appear halfway.
  2. Scene 2 — Devil gates. Pick up a Bible (1000) and carry it to one of the four red gates around the Devil’s nest (another 1000). While you carry a Bible you can breathe fire. Seal all four.
  3. Bonus. No Devil, no enemies, 40 seconds. Step on the glowing arrow tiles to choose which way the maze slides and open the six chests. One holds a dragon egg: an extra life. Getting crushed here only ends the bonus.

Enemies

  • Gloomlet (violet slime): burn it for a toasted puff.
  • Gargling (stone imp, from round 2): fire only freezes it for a moment.
  • Furylet (red slime, from round 7): faster and fiercer.

Gamepads work too (d-pad or stick, any face button fires). In 2-player mode a single gamepad controls player 2. On touch screens use the on-screen pad and FIRE button.

Saving

Pause the game and choose Save Game to store a complete snapshot in one of three browser slots. From the slot list you can Export a slot to a .json file and Import it again later or on another computer.

This remake

Devil’s Maze is an unofficial, non-commercial fan tribute. It contains no code, graphics, music, sound or level data from the original game. The characters (Kindle, Cinder, the Gloomlets, Garglings and Furylets, this Devil and his imps), maze layouts, artwork and music are new and generated procedurally in your browser. “Devil World” and “Nintendo” are trademarks of Nintendo; they are used here only to credit the original work.
